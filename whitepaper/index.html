1. Abstract

1. Abstract

Tokenized equities promise a market that never closes and liquidity that flows without borders. In practice, the market that has emerged looks nothing like that promise. Liquidity for any given tokenized stock is split across multiple pools and venues, price discovery is inconsistent between the token and its real-world underlying, and every order placed on a public chain is visible to anyone running a bot before it even confirms. The result is a market structure that punishes passive holders, rewards extractive actors, and requires constant manual attention to capture opportunities that exist for only seconds at a time.

AgentiFi is an agentic execution layer purpose-built for this environment. Rather than asking users to manually provide liquidity, watch spreads across venues, or track a token's drift from its underlying stock, AgentiFi deploys autonomous agents that do this continuously, and executes every resulting trade through a privacy-preserving pipeline a trusted execution environment for the underlying strategy logic, zero-knowledge eligibility proofs, and a private relayer so that strategy, size, and intent are never exposed on a public mempool.

The thesis is simple: capital should work continuously, and execution should be private by default. This document specifies the three agent strategies that make up the protocol's execution layer, the privacy mechanism that protects every trade those agents place, and the fee and incentive design that aligns agents, liquidity providers, and token holders.

This shift is already underway at the platform level. Robinhood has launched Robinhood Chain, a Layer-2 network carrying 24/7 tokenized Stock Tokens, and has begun rolling out agentic trading — letting users connect AI agents to a dedicated account to trade on their behalf, with plans to extend the same capability into crypto. That trajectory validates the market AgentiFi is built for: tokenized equities that trade continuously, managed increasingly by autonomous agents rather than manual traders. Where platform-level agentic trading is built to execute a user's own strategy on their behalf, AgentiFi is built one layer deeper — as the agentic execution layer for the tokenized-equity market itself, running liquidity, arbitrage, and mean-reversion strategies continuously across pools and venues, and routing every resulting trade through a privacy pipeline no user-facing agent offers today. AgentiFi is a first mover in this specific position: agentic, private execution infrastructure purpose-built for tokenized equities, rather than a general-purpose trading agent applied to them.

2. The Problem

2. The Problem

Tokenized markets are growing. Liquidity isn’t keeping up.

Tokenized equities are being listed across a growing number of chains, pools, and issuers, but the liquidity backing them has not kept pace with that fragmentation. Three structural problems follow directly from this:

Fragmented liquidity:

The same tokenized stock can trade across several pools and venues at once, each with its own depth, fee tier, and price. No single venue reflects a complete picture of the asset's true market price, and capital sitting in any one pool is only ever seeing a fraction of the available flow.

Missed arbitrage:

Because liquidity is fragmented, prices between venues drift apart. Unmanaged exposure:

Concentrated liquidity positions need active range management to stay profitable, and tokenized stocks can temporarily decouple from their real-world underlying during periods when both markets are open. Left unmanaged, liquidity providers absorb impermanent loss during volatility spikes, and price dislocations between token and stock go uncaptured.

Manual strategies cannot operate at the speed or the continuity this market requires. What the market needs instead is capital that is always watching, always ready to rebalance, and always executing — without exposing the trader's hand while doing it.

Every agent strategy is independent at the signal-generation layer — each one watches a different market condition and decides when to act on its own logic — but every trade any agent decides to place converges on the same execution pipeline. This separation matters: it means privacy is not a feature bolted onto one strategy, it is a property of the protocol itself. Whether an agent is rebalancing a liquidity range, executing an atomic arbitrage leg, or entering a mean-reversion position, the trade reaches the chain the same way — decided inside a trusted execution environment that keeps the underlying logic hidden, proved eligible without revealing balance, and routed privately without touching the public mempool.

4. The Agentic Layer

4. The Agentic Layer

AgentiFi runs three agent strategies. They can be deployed independently or run in parallel against the same pool of capital, since each targets a different, largely uncorrelated source of return: trading fees, cross-venue spread, and token-to-underlying convergence.

4.1 Adaptive Liquidity Engine

In plain terms:

Instead of just trading, the agent supplies liquidity directly into the DEX pool for a tokenized stock and earns a cut of every trade that passes through that pool — similar to running a small exchange booth and collecting a fee on every transaction that crosses the counter.

Technical mechanism:

* The agent deposits paired capital (e.g., token + USDC) into an AMM pool.

* It continuously monitors pool utilization and impermanent-loss risk against real-world stock price movement.

* For concentrated-liquidity AMMs (Uniswap v3-style pools), it withdraws and rebalances the liquidity range to stay centered on the current price, maximizing fee capture.

* It can pause automatically if volatility spikes beyond a safe threshold, protecting the position from impermanent loss during a large real-world price move.

Why it's profitable:

Why it's profitable: this is largely passive income once deployed. The agent earns trading fees regardless of market direction, and the ongoing "strategy" work is limited to managing the liquidity range and recognizing when to step back during high volatility. It is a strong complement to the more active strategies below, since it puts otherwise-idle capital to work.

4.2 Cross-Pool Arbitrage

In plain terms:

The same tokenized stock can trade on more than one liquidity pool or DEX — and, as more issuers launch tokenized equities, across more than one issuer as well. Prices between these venues drift apart slightly due to differences in local flow and liquidity. The agent buys where it's cheaper and sells where it's pricier, instantly.

Technical mechanism:

* The agent monitors the same token's price across every available pool and DEX in parallel, via RPC and subgraph queries.

* It computes net profit after gas, fees, and estimated slippage for every pair of venues.

* If net profit clears a minimum threshold, it executes a near-simultaneous buy on one venue and sell on the other — atomic where possible, via a single smart-contract call, to remove execution risk.

Why it's profitable:

There's no prediction involved — it's pure price-matching. It's one of the simplest agent strategies to build (a price-comparison loop plus a threshold check), and the return is close to risk-free as long as execution is atomic. As more liquidity venues for tokenized equities come online, opportunities increase rather than decay.

4.3 Mean Reversion

In plain terms:

During the hours when both the token and the real stock are trading, the token's price can temporarily drift away from the real stock price due to token-market-specific supply and demand. The agent bets that the two converge back together.

Technical mechanism:

* The agent runs a real-time comparison of the token price against the real stock price, sourced via oracle or price feed.

* A statistical model a z-score of the spread flags abnormal deviation.

* The agent takes a position betting on convergence and exits once the spread normalizes.

Why it's profitable:

The token is structurally tied to the value of the real stock it is a claim on, or mirror of, that underlying so large deviations are temporary by design. This makes the strategy closer to a structural arbitrage than a directional bet.

5. Private Execution

5. Private Execution

Every trade generated by the agentic layer — regardless of which strategy produced it — is routed through the same three-stage privacy pipeline before it reaches the chain.

5.1 Zero-Knowledge Proofs of Eligibility

The agent proves it holds sufficient balance and is authorized to trade using a zero-knowledge proof, without revealing the actual wallet balance or identity on-chain. Counterparties and observers can verify that a trade is legitimate without ever learning who placed it or how much capital stands behind it.

5.2 Private Mempool / MEV-Protected Relayer

Orders are submitted through a private transaction relay in the style of Flashbots rather than the public mempool. Bots monitoring pending transactions never see the order before it's confirmed, removing the window in which front-running and sandwich attacks are typically executed.

5.3 TEE Strategy Execution

The actual decision logic — signals, thresholds, weights — runs inside a Trusted Execution Environment, a secure enclave that even the system operators cannot inspect from outside. This means the strategy itself is never exposed, on-chain or off.

Together, these three stages mean an agent can act on a time-sensitive signal — a widening cross-venue spread, a stretched token-to-underlying basis — without broadcasting that signal, or the logic behind it, to anyone watching the chain in real time.

6. Risk Management

6. Risk Management

Autonomy does not mean unmanaged risk. Each agent strategy carries controls specific to the risk it's exposed to:

Volatility guardrails:

The liquidity-provision agent pauses rebalancing automatically when volatility in the underlying stock exceeds a safe threshold, rather than continuing to rebalance into a fast-moving market.

Net-of-cost thresholds:

The arbitrage agent only executes when the spread clears gas, fees, and estimated slippage — it does not chase a spread that would be unprofitable after execution costs.

Statistical confirmation:

The mean-reversion agent only enters a position when the token-to-underlying spread has moved to a statistically abnormal level (via z-score), reducing the chance of entering on ordinary noise rather than a genuine dislocation.

Atomic execution:

Where possible, both legs of an arbitrage trade are executed in a single smart-contract call, removing the risk of one leg filling without the other.

7. Fee Architecture

7. Fee Architecture

AgentiFi's execution layer generates revenue at the point where agents interact with the market:

Liquidity provision

Earns a share of the trading fees generated by the pools the agent supplies.

Arbitrage and mean-reversion

Strategies realize the spread captured on each executed trade.

Protocol Fee

Fee is taken on realized agent profit and routed back into the protocol, funding continued development of the execution and privacy infrastructure and, from Phase 3 onward, rewards distributed to token holders.

The design principle is that the protocol only earns when its agents earn — there is no fee charged on idle or unprofitable capital.

10. Conclusion

10. Conclusion

Tokenized equity markets are expanding faster than the liquidity infrastructure underneath them can keep up with, and that gap is exactly where AgentiFi operates. By combining three complementary agent strategies liquidity provision, cross-venue arbitrage, and token-vs-underlying mean reversion with a privacy pipeline built on trusted-execution-environment strategy logic, zero-knowledge proofs, and a private relayer, AgentiFi lets capital work continuously across a fragmented market without exposing the strategy behind it.

The protocol's roadmap moves deliberately: infrastructure and community first, agentic liquidity and MEV-protected execution second, and full cross-venue arbitrage, mean reversion, and holder rewards third. The result, at full deployment, is a single execution layer where signal and strategy are handled autonomously — and privately from end to end.
